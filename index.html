Rings on the oldest leaves

Early Blight starts on the lower, older leaves as dark brown spots with rings, like a target, and a yellow halo around each one.

Cause
A fungus, Alternaria solani
Spreads by
Soil, water splash and old infected plant debris
Likes
Warm, humid weather, about 24 to 30 °C with humidity above 80%

What to do. Pick off infected lower leaves and destroy them. Water at the root, not over the leaves.

Dark, wet patches that spread in days

Late Blight makes large, water-soaked patches on leaves, with white fuzz underneath in damp weather. On fruit it leaves firm brown rot.

Cause
A water mould, Phytophthora infestans
Spreads by
Spores carried on the wind
Likes
Cool, very humid weather, about 10 to 25 °C with humidity above 85%

What to do. Act the same day. Uproot badly infected plants and keep the leaves dry. Ask your agriculture officer which fungicide to use.

Curled, yellow and stunted

Leaf Curl Virus makes leaves curl upward and turn yellow. Plants stay small and give little fruit.

Cause
A begomovirus
Spreads by
Whiteflies, Bemisia tabaci
Likes
Warm, dry weather when whiteflies multiply, above 28 °C with humidity below 60%

What to do. There is no cure. Remove infected plants, set yellow sticky traps, and use insect nets in the nursery.

From photo to plan

  1. 1

    Take a photo

    Get close to one leaf in good light. Upload the photo, or use the camera on your phone.

  2. 2

    The model reads it

    A MobileNetV2 model, trained on tomato leaf photos from the PlantVillage dataset, picks one of four classes. If it is less than 60% sure, it asks for a clearer photo instead of guessing.

  3. 3

    You get a plan

    The disease name, what to do next in English or Hindi, and a weather risk check for your town.

Honest about doubt

It only knows tomato leaves and four classes. A blurry or non-tomato photo can give a wrong answer, so check with your local agriculture officer before spraying.

Nothing to install

It runs in the browser on any phone or laptop. No account, no download.
